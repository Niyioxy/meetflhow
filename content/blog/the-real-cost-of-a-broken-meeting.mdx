Do the math on your own week. Five meetings a day, thirty minutes average, five people in the room. That's not two and a half hours of your time — it's twelve and a half hours of collective company time, every single day, for one person's calendar alone.

Now ask a harder question: what happened to all of it?

Not the meeting itself — everyone remembers being there. What happened *after*. Who was supposed to send that follow-up? What did you actually agree to on the pricing call three weeks ago? Which action item quietly died because the person who owned it never wrote it down?

That's the real cost of a broken meeting. Not the thirty minutes — the thirty minutes plus everything that evaporates the second everyone hangs up.

## The gap isn't attention. It's memory.

Teams don't lose meetings because people aren't paying attention. They lose them because the only record of what happened lives in someone's head, or in notes nobody reopens, or nowhere at all. The fix isn't "concentrate harder." It's not relying on human memory as your source of truth in the first place.

That's the entire premise behind MeetFlhow: record it once, and let AI do the remembering, structuring, and following up — automatically, every time.

## How it actually works

**Capture, however you already meet.** Record straight from your browser with a live waveform, upload a file from Zoom or a phone recorder, use the Chrome extension on a live Google Meet or Teams call, or record on the go from the mobile app with offline support. No new habit to build — MeetFlhow meets you where the meeting already is.

**Understand, without the manual work.** Every recording comes back with an instant summary, decisions, and action items — with owners and due dates already extracted, ready to become real tasks. A Meeting Coach score tracks engagement, clarity, and balance over time, and a sentiment timeline shows how the tone of the room actually moved, not just where it landed. Speaker identification means the transcript says *who* said something, not just that it was said.

**Live, not just after the fact.** Live captions and live translation (English, Spanish, French, Hindi, Chinese) mean you're not waiting for processing to follow along — including in a language that isn't the room's default.

**Content-aware, because a sermon isn't a sales call.** MeetFlhow analyzes meetings, training sessions, sermons, and podcasts differently — a sermon gets pulled into central themes and reflection questions, not "action items" that were never going to exist in the first place.

**Never lose a recording to a bad connection.** If transcription or analysis fails partway through, retry it with one click. No re-recording, nothing to redo.

**Connect to the tools you already run on.** Push summaries and action items straight into Slack, Notion, Jira, or Linear the moment a meeting finishes processing — or wire up a webhook and route it anywhere else.

**See the bill, and close the loop.** A meeting cost calculator shows what a call actually cost based on attendee salaries — a number that makes "should this have been an email" a lot more concrete. And when it's over, one click drafts a follow-up email summarizing decisions and next steps, instead of a follow-up that never gets written.

**Now, an agent that schedules for you.** The newest piece: tell MeetFlhow who you want to meet with and how long, and it checks your calendar, proposes open times, and lets the other person pick one — no back-and-forth, no double-booking, a real calendar event the moment they choose.

## The point isn't more meetings. It's meetings that hold up their end.

None of this is about making meetings longer, or adding more process on top of an already over-scheduled calendar. It's the opposite: it's making sure the thirty minutes you already spent actually pays off — that the decision gets remembered, the action item gets a name attached to it, and nobody has to reconstruct a conversation from memory three weeks later.
